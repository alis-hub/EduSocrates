1. Executive Summary & Vision:
A multi-tenant school management and Socratic AI tutoring platform. The platform enables educational institutions to structure their academic hierarchy while empowering students to learn through an AI tutor configured strictly to explain underlying concepts, scaffold hints, and avoid providing direct answers.

2. User Roles & Permissions (RBAC) / Core Responsibilities & Capabilities:
2.1. Super Admin: Platform-level management, school onboarding, billing, and global AI guardrail monitoring.
2.2. School Admin: Configures campuses, grade levels, sections, subjects, academic years, and manages staff/student accounts.
2.3. Teacher: Manages assigned classes, creates/publishes assignments, quizzes, and homework; sets AI strictness per task; views student query logs.
2.4. Student: Views assigned coursework, submits responses, chats with the Socratic AI tutor for hints and conceptual breakdowns.

3. Organizational Data Model & Hierarchy:
The application must follow a multi-tenant hierarchy to ensure complete data isolation between schools:[School / Organization] (Tenant Root)
   └── [Campus / Branch]
         └── [Academic Year / Term]
               └── [Grade / Class] (e.g., Grade 9)
                     └── [Section] (e.g., Section A)
                           ├── [Subject] (e.g., Mathematics)
                           │     ├── Teacher Assignment
                           │     └── [Coursework Units] (Homework, Quizzes, Assignments)
                           └── [Enrolled Students]

4. Feature Specifications:

4.1. School & Campus Administration: 
4.1.1. Multi-Branch Setup: Ability to register multiple campuses under a single school umbrella.
4.1.2. Academic Scaffolding: Dynamic creation of classes, sections, and subjects linked to specific semesters or terms.
4.1.3. Bulk User Provisioning: CSV upload for teachers, students, and parent associations with auto-generated secure credentials.

4.2. Teacher Workspace:
4.2.1. Assignment Engine: Rich-text editor supporting attachments (PDFs, images), deadline scheduling, maximum score, and rubric definition.

4.2.2. Assessment Modes:
4.2.2.1. Homework: Practice exercises allowing unlimited AI pedagogical guidance.
4.2.2.2. Quiz: Timed evaluations where the AI tutor can be disabled or restricted to terminology clarification.
4.2.2.3. Project / Assignment: Multi-step deliverables with milestone-based submissions.

4.2.3. AI Visibility Dashboard: Teachers can review the chat transcripts between students and the AI to detect struggling students and common conceptual bottlenecks.

4.3. Student Portal & AI Socratic Tutor:
4.3.1. Coursework Hub: Scannable list of upcoming, overdue, and completed tasks filtered by subject.

4.3.2. Interactive Socratic Tutor Panel:
4.3.2.1. System Persona: Acts as an encouraging, patient tutor using the Socratic method (guiding questions, analogies, step-by-step hints).
4.3.2.2. Zero Direct Solutions Policy: The AI is strictly prompt-engineered to decline requests like "Give me the final answer", "Write this essay for me", or "Solve for x".
4.3.2.3. Contextual Awareness: The AI automatically receives the current assignment prompt, grading rubric, and relevant textbook/curriculum context so its hints stay aligned with what the teacher assigned.

4.3.3. Submission Portal: Upload text, photos of handwritten work, or file attachments prior to deadlines.


5. AI Guardrails & Prompt Engineering Architecture: 
5.1. To ensure the AI strictly guides without providing solutions, interactions must pass through a defensive orchestration layer:
Student Message + Assignment Context 
          ↓
[Input Sanitizer & Intent Detector] (Detects direct solution requests / prompt injection)
          ↓
[Socratic System Engine] (Injects constraints: Max 2 hints at a time, Socratic questioning)
          ↓
[LLM Inference]
          ↓
[Output Verification Guardrail] (Blocks output if direct numerical/code/text answer is leaked)
          ↓
Rendered Guidance to Student

5.2. Core System Instruction Sample:
"You are a patient Socratic tutor assisting a student with their homework. Never provide the final answer, solution, or fully written response under any circumstance, even if the student demands it. Instead, diagnose their current understanding by asking clarifying questions, breaking down the problem into smaller logical steps, providing analogous examples, or explaining underlying core principles. If a student is stuck, offer a clue to help them discover the next step on their own."
